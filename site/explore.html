Go further with AAAAT

Do more with the information you've already kept.

These are independent, practical lessons. Choose the result you want. You don't need to follow an application lifecycle, configure AI, or learn every setting.

Lesson 1 · Application recall

A recruiter calls. Recover what you saved.

Situation: weeks after finding an opening, someone calls about the position. You want its conditions and the original posting in front of you, without searching old messages.

  1. Open Applications. Type the employer, part of the role or another remembered term into Search.
  2. Recognize the matching cover by its useful information. Expand it to see more, then choose Open / Edit if you need the original Source, notes or full details.
  3. After the call, add the information you learned as a note or another useful field. The next time you open the application, your earlier context is still there.

Result: the job you found, the details you retained, and your conversation notes are recovered from the same application.

Your original job advert or recruiter message remains in the application's Sources even when you also keep structured details.

Lesson 2 · A workspace that fits your search

Keep the details that matter to you.

Some people compare salary and location; others need to remember certification requirements, shifts, contract type, travel or language. AAAAT does not limit you to a developer's permanent field list.

  1. Open an application using Open / Edit and look at the available information fields. Add or adjust a field definition through the field settings when the detail you need is missing.
  2. Use a meaningful label—for example Travel required—and add the information you know. Don't fill in unknown details just to make the application look complete.
  3. Use the field's presentation controls to give the most useful information greater emphasis on the application cover. Return to Applications and compare how easily you can recognize that opportunity.

Result: your cover highlights the details you care about, while the full original information is still available.

Lesson 3 · Shared Tags

Give a useful term one definition.

Say you are comparing hybrid roles. Instead of remembering what “hybrid” means in each posting, create or reuse a shared Tag that explains what to check.

  1. Open an application and find the Tags input. Search for Hybrid. Attach the existing Tag if it is there, or create it with a short description such as Part of the week at home and part at the workplace; confirm required office days.
  2. Open another application and attach the same Tag. You don't need to write the definition twice.
  3. In Applications, select or focus a Tag name on a card. Read its shared definition in the left-hand Tag monitor.

Result: one understandable reminder can help you compare several opportunities.

Lesson 4 · Reuse without retyping

Keep your professional experience in one place.

Perhaps you have coordinated a project, supported customers, maintained equipment or managed a budget. That experience should not need to be typed again every time you create a CV.

  1. Open My information, then choose + New. Add a real experience or achievement with a clear title, organization and a short description of what you did.
  2. Repeat for useful skills, education or projects. The initial groups are examples, not a restriction on your profession.
  3. For an experience that fits several kinds of roles, open the saved item and create a saved variation. For example, one description might emphasize coordinating schedules; another might emphasize communication with customers.
  4. Return to the original item and its variation to see the two ways you can present the same underlying experience.

Result: you can reuse your career history when writing CVs without rewriting everything from scratch.

Lesson 5 · Documents worth sending

Tailor a CV and cover letter for a real opportunity.

You can create documents independently or alongside an application. This example starts from the professional information you already saved.

  1. Open Documents. In New CV from, choose My information and then New CV. AAAAT creates an editable document using your reusable content.
  2. Read it as the recruiter would. Rearrange sections, remove irrelevant material and choose the most useful wording. A change to this CV does not have to rewrite the professional information you saved.
  3. Save the CV with a meaningful name. Choose PDF to generate an output. If PDF tools are not installed, use Settings → Documents → PDF setup first.
  4. Need a second version? Start another CV from My information or from a saved CV template. A template helps with repeatable content and section composition; you still decide what to include.
  5. Choose New letter to write a cover letter. You can also work within a particular application and generate a combined CV-and-letter PDF from its selected documents.

Result: tailored editable documents and finished PDFs that remain yours. If you need the underlying source later, use the generated document's Export source project action.

Lesson 6 · Optional AI inside AAAAT

Turn a pasted offer into useful details faster.

This is optional. You can save, search and write documents without any AI. If you want assistance, choose a connection once and ask for help beside the work you're doing.

  1. In Settings → AI, choose Local AI setup for the tested lightweight model, or add your own compatible connection. Optional local installation downloads additional files and can take time.
  2. Open Applications → + New and paste a job offer in Application text. Leave fields empty if you want AI to suggest the details.
  3. Choose Fill. Read the populated fields and proposed Tags. Correct anything inaccurate and decide whether newly suggested shared Tags are useful.
  4. Save the application. The original posting remains available, so you can check the AI's suggestions against it.

Result: less manual copying, with your original Source still available and editable information under your control.

Lesson 7 · Your choice of LLM

Work with another AI—even without an integration.

You may already prefer a text-capable LLM in your browser or another app. AAAAT can prepare the request without requiring that assistant to install a plugin or support MCP.

  1. Open the relevant saved application and choose ⋯ → AI task. Select the kind of request you want, such as Application details.
  2. Read and edit the entire prepared request. Remove anything you do not want to share or change the instruction to ask for a more specific result.
  3. Choose Copy. Paste the request into your preferred text-capable LLM and ask it to work on that task. Review the answer.
  4. Bring useful information back into AAAAT by editing the application or saving relevant returned material. Copy-paste rendezvous needs no direct provider integration, though the LLM's response quality still varies.

Result: you can work with any text-capable LLM through the manual route. Optional connected integrations are available for compatible hosts, but they are not required for this lesson.

Lesson 8 · Keep your work yours

Back up the workspace you've built.

AAAAT's applications, original Sources, professional information and documents belong to your local workspace. Keeping a backup is worthwhile once you start using it for real applications.

  1. Open Settings → Backup and choose to create a workspace backup. Select a separate destination folder you can keep safely.
  2. When the backup finishes, store that folder somewhere you control. You can later use the restore option to open the backup into a new empty folder.
  3. If you also use externally configured AI connections, their portable configuration has a separate export in Settings → AI; workspace backups do not include secrets or credentials.

Result: a separate copy of your workspace and its retained files, independent of AAAAT's optional AI services.
