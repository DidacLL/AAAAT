Inside the workshop

One developer. Substantial AI work. Human direction.

AAAAT is useful software in its own right, and an ongoing experiment in building it with AI assistants.

A modest workshop, an ambitious question

How far can one developer take a coherent product by directing and collaborating with AI agents while keeping control over product meaning and acceptance?

AI contributes real engineering work: investigation, proposals, implementation, code review, research and documentation. It goes well beyond autocomplete.

The human Product Owner defines what AAAAT should do, sets its boundaries, resolves consequential decisions, evaluates the actual experience and decides what ships.

The persuasive answer can still be wrong.

AI-generated code, tests and explanations can look convincing while protecting a bad assumption. A functioning transport does not prove a useful external integration. A green check does not tell us whether an interface makes sense.

AAAAT’s development has included incorrect models, overcomplicated approaches and claims that needed correction. The response is to inspect the product, recover the intended meaning, revise or discard the implementation, and keep useful boundaries where they still serve it.

This is an experiment in human–AI software collaboration. It does not claim that autonomous development is solved or that an AI contribution is reliable because it sounds confident.

Open source means you can look inside.

Users should be able to inspect and modify their tools, keep their information and take their documents elsewhere. Publishing the work also makes the development method and its limitations visible.

Useful criticism, small contributions and reproducible problem reports are welcome. Please use fictional examples in public reports and keep private career documents and credentials out of them.
